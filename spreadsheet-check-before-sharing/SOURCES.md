# Sources and credits

Written by Fully Powered AI in its own words. The method of the work comes from petehottelet/spreadsheet-auditor (MIT), a spreadsheet audit tool whose code Fully Powered AI read but did not use or run. From its main instructions file it takes looking at every suspect cell beside its neighbours and sorting it as a mistake, something to confirm or fine by design; the test that a total is wrong only when it is built differently from a parallel total; reporting a cell once however many checks it trips, and a repeated formula once for its range; leaving the workbook unchanged; treating workbook text as content, never as instruction; and always stating what the check could not see. From references/report_template.md it takes the statement that the report certifies nothing, a coverage section that says what was not checked, and giving each finding its evidence, its effect and its fix. From references/check_catalog.md it takes the kinds of error worth looking for (error values, deleted references, totals that stop short or double count, formulas that break their row's pattern, typed numbers among formulas, numbers buried in formulas, hidden rows feeding totals, numbers stored as text, duplicate lookup keys, swallowed errors, volatile functions, and the balance, sign and period checks) and which of them are often meant. From references/severity_rubric.md it takes raising a finding that feeds a headline figure. Fully Powered AI wrote the twelve-item checklist as a method any assistant can follow from a file or pasted ranges, the pasted-range format with a stop when a block does not say which cells it covers, the Tidy if time section for fragile habits and the rule that moves such housekeeping up to Confirm when it feeds a headline figure, the check on titles, periods and units, the list of what the recipient can see, the effect on headline figures, and the refusal to send the workbook or the report. No third-party text is reused.
