# Year-end tax packet for the accountant

Once the year's books are closed, the owner or bookkeeper hands the accountant one packet: the documents asked for and what is still missing, every contractor paid over the reporting line with missing tax forms flagged, the year's estimated tax payments, and the questions to ask. It works out no tax.

*Workflow template · Finance and accounting · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflow templates/year-end-tax-packet-for-the-accountant/)*

## The outcome

Tax season starts with one organized packet instead of a month of emails back and forth. Your accountant gets the year's headline figures from the closed books, each item from their request letter marked as ready, partly ready or still missing, the contractors paid for services that year with their totals, and the estimated tax payments as you made them. Every assumption the packet relies on is printed on it, so your accountant can see exactly how each figure was put together.

The gaps surface while there is still time to close them. A contractor with no tax details form on file, two payee names that may be one person, a payment made through a platform, a confirmation you cannot find, a document that will arrive after your accountant's date: each is on the page with the facts beside it, and each becomes either an action for you or a question for your accountant.

The packet is not tax advice. It works out no tax owed, no estimated payment and no deduction, and it says nothing about how any item should be treated. Those are your accountant's calls, and the packet sets them out as questions. If a letter from a tax authority is open, the work stops and that letter goes to your accountant first.

## Who starts the work, and when

The owner or the bookkeeper starts it once the year's books are closed and the year-end bank and card accounts are reconciled, usually in the first weeks after the year end, and when the accountant's request letter arrives. It runs again as missing documents and tax details forms come in, until the packet is complete.

The owner approves the packet and sends it to the accountant. The owner or the bookkeeper requests each missing form from the contractor; the assistant contacts nobody.

## What they supply

- **A few settings:** where the business is taxed, the tax year, the business structure and accounting basis as you state them, the date the books were closed, the accounts that hold payments to contractors, and the reporting threshold your accountant gives you.
- **Your accountant's request letter or organizer,** if one arrived. Without one, the packet uses a standard list of what accountants usually ask for.
- **The closed year's profit and loss,** and the cash, card and loan lines of the year-end balance sheet.
- **A vendor payments export** for the year, and your contractor list showing who has a tax details form on file.
- **Your estimated tax payments,** with the period each was for and whether you hold the confirmation.
- **The documents you have gathered,** and a few notes on the year: large purchases, changes, and your own questions.

It works from files and notes you supply, needs no connection to your accounting system, your bank or any tax authority's site, and never asks for a password or a login code. If an export's column headings do not match what it expects, it stops and asks.

## What comes out

One packet in a fixed order: a header that names the tax year and says the packet is not tax advice; the assumptions; the year in figures with two arithmetic checks; the documents for the accountant, item by item; the contractors, with those at or above the threshold, those below it, possible duplicates and the missing forms; the estimated tax payments for the year; the questions for the accountant; and the actions to finish before the packet goes.

A good packet lets your accountant start work the day it arrives, with every figure traced to a line in your own files and nothing left for them to chase except the questions only they can answer.

## What has to change for your company

- **Your reporting rule.** The threshold and form names come from your accountant, for your jurisdiction and your tax year. The template never supplies them from memory.
- **Your accounts.** Which expense accounts hold payments to contractors for services, and the column headings your exports use.
- **Your accountant's list.** Save it each year so the packet follows your accountant's order and wording.
- **Your roles.** Who prepares the packet, who approves and sends it, and who requests missing forms.
- **Your platform.** It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, from files you supply. Decide which exports the assistant may read and where the approved packet is kept.
