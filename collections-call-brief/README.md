# Collections call brief

Before you phone a customer about overdue invoices, one page sets out every open invoice and its age, what they were told and promised, any dispute or credit note still open, who to ask for and the questions to settle. After the call, what was agreed is written up for a person to confirm.

*Workflow template · Finance and accounting · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/collections-call-brief/)*

## The outcome

The person making the call picks up the phone knowing the whole account. Every open invoice is there with its age, oldest first. Every earlier contact is there in the log's own words, and every promise the customer made is marked kept, part kept, broken or still pending, judged against the payments that actually arrived. A payment that turned up in the promised week but went to a different invoice is pointed out, so the call can clear it up instead of tripping over it.

The brief names the contact to ask for and why, and gives a short numbered list of questions, each with its facts: what held up the broken promise, what the open query is really about, when the rest will be paid, where an unapplied payment should go. Disputes get a status question and nothing more. Invoices that are not yet due are left alone. Nothing in the brief threatens the customer or hints at a consequence.

What the caller may offer is quoted word for word from your own written collections terms, sorted into what the caller can agree alone, what needs approval first and what is never offered on a call. Without written terms, the caller offers nothing beyond asking for payment and a date.

After the call, the caller's notes become a call record: the answer to each question, every promise with its date and amounts, anything the customer asked for that goes beyond your terms marked as not agreed, the actions that follow with an owner each, and a contact log entry ready to paste in. Your finance manager or owner confirms it. Nothing is sent, entered or changed by the workflow template.

## Who starts the work, and when

The credit controller, the bookkeeper or the owner runs it before phoning a business customer whose account has overdue invoices: when written reminders have gone unanswered, when a promised payment has not arrived, or when a balance needs settling by phone. The same person runs it again straight after the call with their notes. The finance manager, or the owner in a smaller company, confirms the call record and decides anything beyond the written terms.

It stops, and passes the account to that person, when the customer is an individual rather than a business, when the records mention insolvency, a lawyer or a collections agency, or when the customer has asked not to be phoned.

## What they supply

- **The open items export** for the account from the accounting system: invoices, credit notes and unapplied payments, with dates, amounts and references.
- **The payments received** on the account in the last 90 days, and which invoices each was applied to.
- **The contact log:** earlier calls and emails, with any promise recorded as an amount, a date and the invoices it covers.
- **The customer's contacts,** with the one preferred for payments marked.
- **The disputes file,** with each dispute's amount, reason, status and owner.
- **The collections terms:** your own numbered rules on what a caller may agree, written once and approved.
- **The settings:** the customer, today's date, the caller and the approver, the ledger balance on the account, and the account status.
- **After the call, the caller's notes,** as typed.

When a column heading in an export does not match what the workflow template expects, it stops and asks which column is which.

## What comes out

Before the call, one brief in a fixed order: who to ask for and who else is on file; where the account stands, with a check that the figures add up to the ledger balance; the open invoices, oldest first; the earlier contacts and every promise judged; open disputes, queries never logged as disputes, and unused credit notes and payments; the numbered questions; and what may be offered, quoted from your terms.

After the call, a call record in a fixed order: the answer to each question, or "not covered in the notes"; the customer's promises, checked against your terms; requests beyond the terms, marked not agreed; what the caller said they would do; the actions for a person, each with an owner; what is still open, with the arithmetic; and the contact log entry.

If the open items do not add up to the ledger balance, it stops and shows the difference before drafting anything.

## What has to change for your company

Write your collections terms once, as numbered items your finance manager or owner approves: how far out a caller may accept a promised date, who approves instalments or later dates, what is never offered on a call, and what may be sent on request. Name your caller and your approver. Map your accounting system's exports to the column headings the workflow template expects. Set how many days' grace a promised payment gets before it counts as missed, and how far back to read the contact log. Keep the contact log in the shape the call record writes, so each confirmed entry can be pasted straight in.

It is the step before and after a phone call. Written reminders by age are [overdue-invoice follow-up preparation](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/overdue-invoice-follow-up-preparation/); matching short or unexplained payments is a [receivables exception review](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/receivables-exception-review/); the weekly picture for management is the [weekly receivables summary](https://fullypowered.ai/workflows/weekly-receivables-summary/). It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini from the files you supply and needs no access to any system.
