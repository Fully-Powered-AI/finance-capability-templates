# Thirteen-week cash flow forecast

The owner sees cash week by week for the next thirteen weeks: what comes in, what goes out, the lowest point and every week below the floor they set, with each assumption written on the page. The forecast informs the decision; the owner makes it, and nothing is paid or moved.

*Workflow template · Finance and accounting · version 0.1.0 · [Workflows Directory](https://fullypowered.ai/workflow templates/thirteen-week-cash-flow-forecast/)*

## The outcome

The owner stops guessing whether the money will be there. One page shows the bank balance today and where it lands at the end of each of the next thirteen weeks, built line by line from the invoices you have sent, the orders you have won, the bills you owe and the payments you make every month.

The weeks that need attention stand out: the lowest point and what causes it, any week that falls below the cash floor you choose, any week that goes below zero. Two more lines answer the questions owners lie awake over: how the weeks look if nothing new is sold, and how they look if the two customers you depend on most each pay four weeks behind their usual pace.

Every assumption behind the numbers is printed on the forecast, so anyone reading it can see exactly what it rests on and challenge it. The forecast gives you the facts for the decision. Whether to chase a payment, move a purchase, draw on credit or wait is yours to decide, and nothing is paid, moved or sent.

## Who starts the work, and when

The bookkeeper, office manager or finance lead runs it every Monday, from the bank balance at Friday's close and fresh exports from the accounting system. In a smaller company the owner runs it themselves. It is also worth running before any large commitment, such as new equipment, a new hire or a big order that needs materials up front.

The owner or finance lead reads it and decides what, if anything, to do. Where the forecast will back a loan, a payment to the owners or a decision to take on staff, the company's accountant reviews it first.

## What they supply

- **The opening balance:** cash in the business accounts, the date it was read and who read it. The forecast will not run without it.
- **Invoices you have sent and not been paid for,** exported from the accounting system.
- **Invoices paid over the last year,** so each customer's real payment habits set when their money is expected.
- **Orders you have won but not yet invoiced,** with what makes each one firm. Hoped-for deals can be listed too; they are shown and never counted.
- **Bills you owe,** exported from the accounting system.
- **Regular payments and receipts:** payroll from your payroll records, rent, loans, subscriptions and anything collected on a monthly plan.
- **One-off items you already know about:** a tax payment, an equipment deposit, materials for the work you have won, the next card statement.
- **Your floor:** the lowest balance you are comfortable holding.

It works from files and notes you supply. No connection to your bank or accounting system is needed, and none is asked for.

## What comes out

One forecast in a fixed order:

1. **Where it stands,** in one line: above or below zero and the floor, and the lowest point.
2. **The grid:** thirteen weeks across, receipts and payments by kind down the side, closing cash at the foot of each week, then closing cash with no new sales and with the two largest customers paying four weeks late.
3. **What the grid shows:** the lowest point and its cause, the weeks below the floor and below zero, and the two cases.
4. **The assumptions,** numbered, each with the weeks it affects.
5. **Every receipt and payment placed,** with the date it is expected and why.
6. **What was not counted,** and why: an invoice long past its usual timing, money expected after week thirteen, deals not yet won.
7. **The checks:** the arithmetic from opening to closing cash, and every row of every export accounted for.
8. **Questions for the owner:** payments that often go missing from a forecast, such as insurance or sales tax, and regular payments that appear in last quarter's bills but on no list.
9. **The decisions this forecast informs,** written as facts with no recommendation.

## What has to change for your company

- **Your floor.** The balance below which you want to know in advance. If you do not set one, the forecast uses the largest regular payroll payment.
- **Your week.** Which day a week starts, and the day you run it.
- **What counts as won.** A signed order, a purchase order or a deposit received. Anything less stays out of the totals.
- **Your payment rhythm.** Payroll dates, rent and loan dates, tax dates and the card statement date.
- **Your exports.** The column names your accounting system produces, so they are read exactly as they come.
- **Who reviews it.** Who sees the forecast each week, and when your accountant is brought in.
- **Your platform.** It runs in ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini. Decide which files the assistant may read.
