# Sources and credits

Written by Fully Powered AI in its own words. The way of working draws on two published methods, both Apache-2.0. From the pricing method in CaseMark/skills: starting from the margin each product line earns today, looking at the gap between the price on the list and the price customers actually pay, setting the proposal beside competitors' published prices and treating an undated or stale price with suspicion, working each proposed price through to sales, units and gross profit in money and as a percentage with the cost assumption stated, finding how far units can fall before gross profit drops below today's, and keeping in view customer commitments that limit when a new price can apply. From the margin method kept in the archive of CarvalhoJeo/claude-small-business-br, which came from an earlier version of Anthropic's small-business plugin: building each item's figures from what was really collected and what each unit costs, refusing a cost of zero and asking for a cost per item, including the owner's own time for a service, showing how much margin rising costs have taken while prices stood still, testing several assumed falls in units when there is too little history to go on, treating a single earlier price rise as one observation and no more, a closing note on the limits of the data, and leaving the choice of price entirely to the owner. Fully Powered AI added the default export headers with a stop when a header does not match, the rule that a competitor price counts only with the date and place it was seen and an age limit after which it is set aside, the totals across all items at each response, the margin floor the owner sets, the rounding rules, and the questions for the owner. Nothing is changed, sent or published. No third-party text is reused.
