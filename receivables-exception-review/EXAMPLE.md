# Receivables exception review: an invented example

*Illustrative example with invented data.* One week at an invented 60-person professional-services firm, after five receipts totalling \$16,100.

**Receivables exceptions for the controller: 25 September 2026**

- **Period:** Receipts 21 to 24 September. Ledger as of 25 September. Bank feed to 24 September.
- **1. Short payment:** Invoice 1042, Client A, \$1,200. Check for \$1,000 on 22 September, memo “Inv 1042”, applied by the billing coordinator under the remittance rule; \$200 open. No agreed discount in the notes. Possible: short payment, a discount the client expects, a second payment in transit. Next step: billing coordinator asks Client A which applies; hold any reminder. Decides: controller.
- **2. Ambiguous receipt:** Wire for \$5,600 on 23 September, no reference, payer not on file. Candidates: 1044 (Client D) and 1045 (Client F), each \$5,600, each due 20 September. Neither account lead’s notes mention a payment. Next step: hold unapplied; billing coordinator asks the bank for originator details; both account leads check with their clients; hold reminders on both. Decides: controller.
- **3. Unmatched receipt:** \$350 from Client J on 24 September, reference “retainer top-up”. No open invoice; no retainer terms in the files. Next step: Client J’s account lead confirms whether a retainer was agreed. Decides: controller.
- **4. Disputed invoice:** Invoice 1035, Client E, \$3,800, 42 days past due. Note of 2 September: client queries phase-two hours. Not chased. Next step: account lead reports on the dispute. Decides: controller, on any credit.
- **Checked, nothing found:** Duplicates: none among the five receipts or against postings from the last 30 days.
- **Could not assess:** Whether Client A has a second payment in transit: the bank feed ends 24 September. Who sent the \$5,600 wire.

Prepared for the controller to review. No receipt has been applied, written off or credited by this list.
